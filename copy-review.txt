==============================================================================
CHAPTER 1 · THE ISSUE
==============================================================================
When does recognized rent stop being income?
A landlord books rent evenly over a lease (straight-line) and can lend money to the tenants who pay it. Both let reported earnings run ahead of cash. The question is whether MPW’s did — and whether it recognized in time that its tenants couldn’t pay.
On Jan 26, 2023, short-seller Viceroy Research alleged that MPW was lending tenants the money to pay its rent.

  Income statement: Straight-line rent revenue; Reserves booked as negative revenue
  Balance sheet: Straight-line rent receivable; Loans to tenants; Allowance for credit losses (ACL)
  Cash flow statement: Net income vs. cash flow from operations (CFO); The “straight-line rent” add-back
  Footnotes: Tenant concentration; Moves to cash basis; Write-offs

  [explainer] Contractual cash rent / Straight-line revenue / Receivable / Yr / Illustrative lease
  Revenue now, cash later. The receivable is a bet that the tenant lasts.

CHAPTER 3 · WHO PERFORMED BETTER?
==============================================================================
OHI earned more, more steadily, with cash to show for it.
ROE = margin × turnover × leverage

  MPW FY2022: ROE 10.6% = 58.5% × 0.077× × 2.36×
  MPW FY2023: ROE −6.9% = −63.8% × 0.046× × 2.34×
  MPW FY2024: ROE −38.7% = −242.1% × 0.061× × 2.61×
  OHI FY2022: ROE 10.8% = 48.6% × 0.092× × 2.41×
  OHI FY2023: ROE 6.4% = 25.5% × 0.103× × 2.45×
  OHI FY2024: ROE 9.6% = 38.6% × 0.111× × 2.24×
  SBRA FY2022: ROE −2.4% = −12.4% × 0.107× × 1.82×
  SBRA FY2023: ROE 0.5% = 2.1% × 0.116× × 1.90×
  SBRA FY2024: ROE 4.6% = 18.0% × 0.132× × 1.93×

1. OHI ROE 10.8% → 6.4% → 9.6%; positive every year, CFO above net income every year.
2. MPW ROE collapses 10.6% → −6.9% → −38.7% as Steward unwinds; CFO falls three years running ($812M → $246M).
3. SBRA’s losses (FY2021–22) are mostly joint-venture (JV) impairments; FY2022 also reflects real-estate impairments ($94.0M) and a tenant write-off ($17.1M). Lowest leverage (1.82×–1.93×), improving.
Verdict: OHI > SBRA > MPW

CHAPTER 4 · EARNINGS VS. CASH
==============================================================================
[badge] Pre-event window: FY2021–FY2023 · Outcome window: FY2024
In FY2022, MPW’s earnings rose while its cash fell.
Depreciation is a large non-cash expense, so a landlord’s net income normally sits below its cash flow from operations (CFO): accruals (net income − CFO) are normally negative.

[beat 1] Total accruals (net income − CFO)
  MPW: FY2021 −$155.7M | FY2022 +$163.6M | FY2023 −$1.06B
  OHI: FY2021 −$305.4M | FY2022 −$198.8M | FY2023 −$375.5M
  SBRA: FY2021 −$469.7M | FY2022 −$393.3M | FY2023 −$286.8M
  marker: MPW FY2022 −$373.2M ex-gain
  labels: Below zero is normal · MPW FY2022 turned positive (+$163.6M) only through a $536.8M gain on property sales, mainly the Macquarie sale; −$373.2M without it · reserves & impairments
[beat 2] MPW: net income vs. CFO
  Net income: FY2021 $656M | FY2022 $903M | FY2023 −$557M
  CFO: FY2021 $812M | FY2022 $739M | FY2023 $506M
  In FY2022, net income rises (+37.6%) while CFO falls (−9.0%).
[beat 3] What explains the gap · MPW FY2022
  Gain on property sales $536.8M
  Straight-line rent and other non-cash revenue $282.5M
  Offset: impairments $268.4M
  SBRA FY2021–22: large negative accruals from impairments and write-offs while CFO held at $356M → $316M. The cash was fine; the assets were marked down. MPW FY2023–24 is different: its cash fell too.
[beat 4] What this test cannot see
  Rent paid out of the landlord’s own loan still counts in CFO; the loan sits in investing. Accruals cannot detect round-tripping.
  Harshest case, CFO less every new loan advanced: $753M → $532M → $256M (reported $812M → $739M → $506M).

CHAPTER 6 · THE VERDICT
==============================================================================
Overstated? Yes, in substance. Fabricated? No evidence.

  Signal 1 · Cash flows — Earnings outran cash.
    FY2022 net income +37.6%, CFO −9.0%; CFO then fell every year to FY2024. Non-cash straight-line rent was 21–26% on top of every dollar of rent billed (FY2021–22).
  Signal 2 · Balance sheet and footnotes — Losses were recognized late.
    Allowance 2–4% of the loans and financing leases it covered vs OHI’s 10–13% of loans; Steward’s revenue share given only as more than 10%; Steward moved to cash basis only after it stopped paying in full. Then $2.28B of Steward charges in FY2023–24.
  Paper rent, literally.
    In 2023, MPW recognized about $82M of revenue from Prospect, a tenant already on cash basis, paid in equity of Prospect’s own managed-care business, “in lieu of cash.”
  [Strongest fact against us]
    A Wachtell Lipton review, engaged by the Audit Committee of MPW’s board, found no evidence of round-tripping. The first Steward shortfall MPW disclosed was Sep 2023 rent, after about $2.00B of rent and interest paid since 2016.
    Response: This narrows our claim; it doesn’t reverse it. GAAP-compliant timing choices can still overstate economic performance. The issue is when losses were recognized, not whether revenue was invented.

==============================================================================
CHAPTER 7 · HOW WE USED AI
==============================================================================
AI did the retrieval. We did the judgment.

  Helpful
    • Pulled all 12 10-Ks and the SEC XBRL data in minutes
    • Full-text search across filings for “cash basis,” “straight-line,” concentration
    • Built and checked the ratio workbook from one data file
  Bottlenecks
    • SEC blocks automated requests without a User-Agent; our cloud tool was blocked, so we went through a browser
    • XBRL misses company-specific tags (MPW’s straight-line receivable, tenant concentration) → back to footnotes
    • Restated comparatives vs as-filed numbers; MPW’s 10-Ks gave no Steward revenue figure before FY2024
  Learned
    • AI is fastest at finding, weakest at defining (what counts as a “loan to a tenant”?)
    • Every ratio needed a human definition decision
    • Absence of disclosure is itself a data point

4. Definitions and choices
    • Equity: total equity including noncontrolling interests.
    • Debt: carrying value (net), sum of all borrowings.
    • SBRA comparable revenue: rental + interest, excluding resident fees ($414M → $438M → $411M → $419M).
    • MPW “loans to tenants”: mortgage loans + other loans + loan-type investments in unconsolidated operating entities.
    • MPW’s allowance also covers financing leases. Supplementary ratio on loans + financing leases: 1.5% → 3.6% → 3.6% → 27.8%.
    • OHI largest-tenant revenue: implied from the disclosed percentage.
